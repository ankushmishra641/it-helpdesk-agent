> Fictional Northstar Systems policy for this public demonstration. Not instructions for a real employer.

## Symptoms
The employee no longer has access to their enrolled authenticator device.

## Prerequisites
Use a managed Northstar demo device and the approved work account. Check for an active service incident before changing local settings. Public demo actions never affect real accounts.

## Resolution
1. Confirm the reported symptom: The employee no longer has access to their enrolled authenticator device.
2. Establish scope: affected user count, managed asset, location and time of onset. Check the identity service snapshot.
3. Guide the employee through the approved steps: Report the lost device through the service desk. Use an existing recovery method only through the approved sign-in page. Do not enroll a replacement phone through an unsolicited link. Submit an Identity request describing the lost factor without sharing credentials.
4. Record which check changed the outcome. Do not mark resolved until the employee confirms the affected workflow works.
5. If unresolved: An authorized Identity administrator must perform recovery after verified identity checks; manager approval alone is not technical verification.

## Validation
Repeat the original affected workflow once. Record whether it succeeds, the service involved and the time. If it still fails, keep the ticket open and document the exact remaining symptom.

## Escalation
An authorized Identity administrator must perform recovery after verified identity checks; manager approval alone is not technical verification.
Use High priority for multi-user disruption or suspected compromise, Medium for one blocked employee, and Low for a routine request with a workaround.

## Evidence and privacy
Record the asset identifier, error code and redacted diagnostic summary. Never collect passwords, recovery keys, MFA codes, private documents or full message contents.
